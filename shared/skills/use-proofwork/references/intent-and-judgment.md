# Intent, judgment and engineering detail

Read when the request is expressed through an experience, a creative correction or an unfamiliar technical choice. Apply the shared skill-compression principle: the coordinator carries engineering detail while the user steers the outcome.

## Convert ordinary intent into something observable

Inspect the product before questioning the user. Translate "make this feel calmer" into a provisional direction grounded in the actual audience, content hierarchy and interaction: reduce competing emphasis, improve reading rhythm, keep important recovery information visible. Explain the intended change in everyday language and build a bounded slice when authorized. Do not make the person name typography techniques or individual design skills. If no rendered inspection is available, disclose that limit and treat the visual direction as provisional.

For "it should save reliably," trace the actual write and recovery path. Propose criteria such as a saved change surviving reload and an interrupted attempt presenting an accurate state. Choose the implementation and verification techniques yourself. Clarify the consequence that cannot be inferred, such as whether several people must edit the same item simultaneously; do not require the user to design locking or retry algorithms.

## Ask about consequences, recommend the mechanism

When a technical choice matters, connect it to what the person will experience or operate. "Will several people edit the same record at once?" can change storage and conflict handling. "Which database do you want?" hands back a decision the plugin should first investigate. A supplied technical preference remains an explicit constraint; do not replace it under the pretext of simplicity.

Give the simplest supported recommendation and explain its relevant tradeoff. Separate repository facts from assumptions. Ask for a missing decision when it changes cost, privacy, durability, access or authorized delivery. Do not silently accept a paid service, weaker recovery target or destructive migration because it keeps the conversation short. Never equate fewer questions with permission.

## Make revision concrete

Return a reviewable slice tied to the person's goal: an exercised export, an observed interaction, a rendered page or a clearly labeled proposal when execution is unavailable. Invite feedback through the normal conversation without blocking already authorized independent work. A correction such as "warmer, less corporate" updates the visual/copy criteria; it does not discard functioning validation or expand publication authority.

Keep expert control accessible. If the user asks why a mechanism exists, trace the real reason and offer the relevant evidence or code. Progressive disclosure means detail is available when useful, not hidden behind a confident summary. Default updates describe the outcome, the consequence of a decision and what observation will settle the remaining uncertainty.

## Deliver a result someone can judge

Explain what changed for the person, what was actually exercised and what remains uncertain. Show an actionable artifact or evidence where available. A screenshot supports a specific appearance; it does not prove persistence or authorization. Avoid replacing missing runtime evidence with the phrase "production ready."

For evaluation, separate instructional scenarios from observed agent behavior. Compare a novice brief and an expert brief for equivalent outcomes and preserved constraints; record technical choices unnecessarily delegated to the person and consequential choices correctly surfaced. Compression succeeds when the person needs less mechanism-specific knowledge while the delivered result remains inspectable and verified.
