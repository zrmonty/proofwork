# Brainstorm

Help the person decide what is worth building. Carry the exploration yourself: use their ordinary language, inspect existing context and propose something concrete they can judge. A new project needs a direction, not an immediate stack prescription.

## Locate the unresolved decision

Read the brief, repository instructions, existing product, supplied references and any accepted plan. Identify the audience, intended change in their lives, main journey and practical constraints. Separate what the person supplied from your assumptions. If a direction is already accepted and the request is to implement it, return to that stage rather than reopening settled product decisions.

For an open idea, ask only material questions whose answers change the direction. Favor a question about a person's actual workflow or desired experience over a feature checklist. Continue useful exploration while answers are pending. When context is adequate, state a provisional assumption and proceed within scope; silence does not establish approval or permission for consequential effects.

## Explore a useful choice

Describe a concrete journey: who arrives, what they need, what they do and what a useful result looks like. Offer a few materially different directions only when they expose a real choice. Explain the tradeoff in experience, scope or operating responsibility and recommend a fit grounded in the brief. Avoid generating interchangeable ideas or mandatory option counts.

Make differences tangible with a realistic content sketch, example interaction, simple scenario or authorized bounded prototype. Label invented content and untested claims. A product brief does not establish demand, a prototype does not prove durability, and textual visual direction does not count as rendered evidence.

For interfaces, load frontend-design to develop a content-specific direction and humanizer for relevant application wording. Preserve the user's taste and supplied references without reproducing another product's composition mechanically. Technical feasibility may constrain the idea; investigate the specific uncertainty without choosing a whole stack prematurely.

## Turn direction into the next stage

Produce a concise working brief: intended audience/outcome, primary journey, first useful slice, explicit exclusions, meaningful constraints, unresolved assumptions and observable acceptance. Distinguish an exploratory recommendation from a user-confirmed direction. Preserve enough context for a later correction without making the person manage a design document.

If the next unresolved decision is tools, boundaries, persistence or operating ownership, hand this brief to architecture-fit. If those decisions are already adequate and implementation is requested, return the accepted constraints to the root for the feature or bug-fix playbook. A brainstorming-only request ends with the useful direction; it does not authorize code, purchases, publication or a migration.

Do not require a ceremonial approval for every reversible detail. Do pause dependent work when a missing choice genuinely changes the result or scope. Creative iteration can revisit the direction, but keep settled constraints and unaffected evidence unless the correction invalidates them.
