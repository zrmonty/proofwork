# Composition and visual decisions

Use this reference while choosing a direction or correcting a layout whose content could be replaced with any other product's text. These are decision examples, not templates to impose.

## Choose a structure from the content

| Product shape | Useful hierarchy | A revealing failure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Local essay publication | Lead story, recent writing, author context, readable article detail | Every excerpt gets equal weight, while bylines and reading actions disappear |
| Working catalog | Search/filter, comparable results, salient attributes, item detail | Decorative heroes push comparison below the first useful view |
| Operations console | Status, actionable queue, exceptions, detail and recovery | Oversized promotional headings or cards conceal task density |
| Independent service | Concrete offer, credible work, process, contact action | Invented social proof substitutes for evidence and personality |
| Small personal tool | Input, result, understandable feedback | A dashboard shell surrounds one useful control |

An editorial index might choose a lead essay with a quieter text list, or a compact chronological stream with a strong masthead and selective imagery. Those directions change how a reader discovers content. A task console might choose a master/detail queue or a focused review sequence. Explain which better serves scanning, decisions, or sustained reading under the actual brief.

## Typography carries structure

Choose roles before font names: identity, headline, body, metadata, controls, and dense data. Most roles can share a family and differ through weight, scale, spacing, or case. An expressive face must remain readable with the supplied longest title. Body text should support sustained reading at the chosen width; check actual paragraphs and links, not a specimen sentence.

Use the incumbent font stack when it suits the direction. If a different family materially improves the identity, check license, availability, fallback, and loading behavior. Avoid relying on a font that only exists on the developer's machine. Inspect line breaks with the fallback as well as the preferred face. Avoid a prescribed serif/sans pairing: a publication can be precise and contemporary without a serif, and a business tool can have character without decorative display type.

Metadata needs legibility even when it is secondary. Hierarchy comes from relationships; reducing text until readers struggle is not a hierarchy strategy. Keep headline, byline, summary, and action grouped by meaning. Avoid letterspacing or all-caps as a blanket fix for weak differentiation.

## Rhythm, density, and shape

Start with one repeated spacing relationship: within an item, between related items, between sections. Reuse it to make grouping predictable, then vary deliberately where the content warrants a pause. Check baselines and edges across adjacent regions. Randomly large gaps make a page feel unfinished; uniformly tight gaps erase grouping.

Give text and images widths that suit their jobs. A long article need not occupy the full browser width; a comparison table may need the space. Inspect how image crop and focal point change at narrow sizes. Avoid inventing a photo merely to decorate a section that communicates better through text or data.

Choose a small set of radius roles and inspect them on real controls and surfaces. Softer media can complement open text; nested rounded panels often add visual noise without structure. Borders, background changes, and whitespace can establish containment with less weight than a card. Keep contrast and disabled/focus distinctions coherent across these treatments.

## Distinction through a few durable choices

Useful character can come from an unusually clear masthead, a specific crop strategy, generous article rhythm, a disciplined accent, an expressive but readable title scale, or meaningful editorial ordering. Carry the choice across index, detail, and state views. Do not sprinkle unrelated motifs to prove originality.

When refining an existing product, identify what users already recognize and keep it. Correct hierarchy, typography, rhythm, or state clarity within that identity. A new palette and rewritten components may exceed a request to make a page more appealing.
