# Detailed humanizer workflow

Read the applicable sections for the current transformation, restructuring, interface or writing decision. These details do not create a mandatory workflow for every small edit.

# Humanizer

Read [messages as contracts](../references/message-contracts.md) when wording summarizes partial results, spans surfaces or contains localized/dynamic values. Keep the observed event authoritative while choosing a useful full message for each surface.

Make the application sound as though someone understands the person using it and the task in front of them. Useful human writing is specific, accurate and appropriately warm. It does not need a personality in every sentence. A clear label can do more than a paragraph of welcome copy.

Use this skill when creating or revising visible application wording: navigation, page introductions, forms, buttons, empty states, errors, confirmations, notifications and help. Use it alongside frontend-design for page composition. A request to humanize copy does not authorize changing product behavior, removing required information or rewriting unrelated technical documents.

## Understand the person and the moment

Read the real screen, its surrounding flow, existing language, product terminology and relevant content constraints. Identify what the person knows, what they are trying to do and whether this moment is routine, uncertain, frustrating or consequential. The same voice can be calm during an error and welcoming on a publication page without becoming two different brands.

Preserve supplied facts, prices, limits, permissions, legal meaning and actual system capabilities. Distinguish fixtures from published claims. Do not invent customer quotes, first-person experience, popularity, urgency, guarantees or familiarity to make copy feel authentic. Preserve required attribution and product disclosures. Natural writing is a quality of the text, not a claim about who authored it.

Infer the audience and appropriate register from the repository and supplied brief. Ask only when an unresolved audience, terminology or behavior question changes the wording. Keep dependent copy provisional while improving independent parts. A small label repair needs a small decision; it does not need a voice workshop.

For a new or inconsistent application, record a short voice note: who reads it; the ordinary vocabulary they use; the desired relationship; and a few representative action, help and error lines. Favor observable choices such as “name the failed action and offer its actual recovery” over personality adjectives alone. Follow an established suitable voice rather than imposing casualness everywhere.

## Give every piece of copy a job

Read [structure and voice](../references/structure-and-voice.md) when shaping a page or diagnosing boilerplate. Classify the visible content by purpose: orientation, information, action, navigation or recovery. Remove repeated explanations and decorative labels before adding another region. Keep the facts someone needs to decide, especially around cost, access, data loss and next steps.

Use headings where they separate meaningful topics or make a long page easier to scan. Do not add a heading, eyebrow, subtitle and explanatory paragraph to every control group. A short settings screen may need its page title, direct field labels and one relevant note. A long guide may need several well-named headings. Preserve semantic heading order and useful accessible landmarks when simplifying visual chrome.

Treat the site header and footer as functional regions. Keep navigation, account controls, required links and useful orientation. Avoid duplicating navigation, adding a full marketing footer to an in-app task, or ending every screen with a slogan and another call to action. A publication may genuinely need masthead, archive links and credits; a checkout needs focus. Choose from the page's job rather than a fixed quota of sections.

Reduce artificial repetition across the whole journey, not just one screen: the same welcome message, success celebration or promise need not recur in every panel. Keep deliberate terminology consistent even while sentence rhythm varies. Never trade clarity for novelty by giving the same action a different name on each page.

## Write plain, specific language

Prefer concrete nouns and active verbs. Name what the person will get or change. Replace unsupported praise, vague transformation and stock introductions with the actual information. “Export invoices” tells more than “Unlock seamless financial insights.” When there is no useful information to replace a sentence, deletion may be the best edit.

Use contractions when they fit the established voice and context. Mix sentence lengths naturally; avoid a repeated pattern of three benefits, a dramatic contrast and a closing slogan. Read the copy aloud to find stiffness, but do not add filler, deliberate errors, forced jokes, slang or personal anecdotes merely to imitate conversation. Use specialist vocabulary when the audience needs it and ordinary language elsewhere.

Avoid addressing every action with enthusiasm. Routine success can be “Changes saved.” A failed payment needs a clear state and recovery, not a cheerful exclamation or a vague apology. Remove unearned claims such as effortless, revolutionary or perfect unless the supplied context genuinely supports their meaning. Evaluate the sentence in context; a prohibited-word list cannot judge voice.

Keep sentences near the action or fact they explain. Prefer connected prose for a short explanation, a list for genuinely parallel options, and a table for comparison. Do not replace every paragraph with bullets or every pair of sentences with a heading. Formatting should help someone find information, not expose the writer's template.

## Make interaction copy accurate

Read [interaction copy and review](../references/interaction-copy-and-review.md) when changing controls, states or localized strings. Action labels describe the real effect. Use “Save draft” when saving a draft, “Send invitation” when sending, and the necessary object when “Delete” alone is ambiguous. Keep visible labels and accessible names aligned. Preserve recognizable navigation vocabulary.

For an error, establish what is known, what happened to the person's work and what they can actually do next. Distinguish empty data from a filter with no matches and a failed request. Do not say “Nothing here yet” when loading failed, promise an undo that does not exist, or tell someone to retry a payment when its outcome is uncertain. Coordinate consequential wording with the implementation's observed state.

Write form labels that stand on their own; placeholders are examples rather than the only labels. Put necessary format or consequence guidance before an avoidable error. Make confirmations proportionate: clearly state the affected object and real consequence; omit filler. Keep useful accessibility, security, privacy and consent wording even when it is less conversational.

Preserve localization keys, interpolation contracts, plurals, escaping and locale formatting. Avoid stitched sentence fragments, invented string keys or shortened labels that lose context. Do not silently change approved legal text; identify the proposed simplification and its unresolved meaning when review is needed. Content-only work need not introduce new frameworks or a suite of tests that merely freezes incidental phrasing.

## Review the experience and finish

Check the same action across its controls, confirmations, toasts and notifications for conflicting promises. Preserve the public disclosure policy and avoid exposing internal diagnostics or private data through friendlier errors. Use the interaction reference when audience, privacy or cross-surface state changes what can truthfully be said.

Read the full flow after editing. Check repetition, terminology, promises, heading density and whether the next action is easy to understand. Compare representative ordinary and failure states. A sentence can sound natural in isolation and become irritating when repeated ten times.

When changing an implemented interface, inspect its rendered context where tools are available: narrow layouts, long labels, meaningful heading order, error placement and focus/accessible-name behavior affected by the edit. Exercise changed actions if wording depends on their effect. Reuse existing checks for behavior or localization contracts. Do not claim rendered or interaction verification from source inspection alone; a wording-only proposal can be reviewed as text with that scope stated.

Return the revised copy or working change, a concise account of material structural edits, what was verified and any facts or states still requiring confirmation. Finish when the text is accurate, fits its real place and helps the person understand or act without unnecessary explanation. Do not claim an AI-detector score or human-authorship guarantee; judge the reader's experience and the product's contract.
