# Worked revisions with their facts attached

Use these as editorial reasoning examples, not a replacement string library. Each revision depends on the
stated product facts. When those facts differ, preserve the real behavior rather than copying the example.

## A generic promise becomes a useful instruction

Facts: the tool exports invoices as CSV for a selected date range.

Before: "Unlock a seamless reporting experience. Effortlessly harness the power of your financial data."

After: "Export invoices. Download a CSV for the date range you choose."

Reason: the supported action and format replace praise. If the screen already makes both obvious, a labeled
date range and "Export CSV" control may be enough. Do not add an explanatory paragraph automatically.

## A formal product remains formal

Facts: only workspace owners may invite members; "owner" is the established role name.

Before: "Invitation functionality is exclusively available to users possessing workspace ownership privileges."

After: "Only workspace owners can invite members."

Reason: clearer grammar preserves the restriction and role. "Grab an owner and get your crew onboard!"
would impose a new register and make the instruction less precise.

## Queued work stays queued

Facts: a report request is accepted into a queue; progress appears in Exports; email delivery is unsupported.

Before: "Your report is ready! We'll send it straight to your inbox."

After: "Report queued. Check its progress in Exports."

Reason: the original is inaccurate, not merely overenthusiastic. The destination and control must actually
open Exports. A wording edit cannot turn a download button into navigation.

## An uncertain outcome does not become reassurance

Facts: payment status is unknown after a timeout; a supported Payments page can reconcile the transaction.

Before: "Something went wrong. Don't worry, you haven't been charged. Try again."

After: "We couldn't confirm the payment. Check Payments before paying again."

Reason: the rewrite preserves uncertainty and avoids encouraging a duplicate effect. It does not assert a
charge or a refund. Different recovery capabilities require different wording.

## Help replaces performed empathy

Facts: a save failed; the form retains entered values; retry is safe.

Before: "We understand how frustrating this must be! Oops, that didn't quite work."

After: "Your changes weren't saved. They're still in this form; try again."

Reason: known outcome and retained work provide useful reassurance without guessing feelings. If retention
is unverified, omit that claim and report the missing fact instead of making the error sound comforting.

## Remove a repeated content layer

Facts: an account page already has a title, labeled notification controls, shared navigation and required links.

Before: "YOUR PREFERENCES / Shape your experience / Take control of what matters / Notification preferences."

After: "Notifications" followed by the existing controls and any necessary explanation of their effects.

Reason: keep orientation, unique help, navigation and required links. Removing a redundant local introduction
does not authorize deleting a shared footer or accessible landmark across unrelated screens.

## Specificity survives the shorter version

Facts: retained files expire after 30 days; owners may delete them earlier.

Before: "Your files remain available for a generous period, with flexible management options."

After: "Files are kept for 30 days. Owners can delete them sooner."

Reason: the period and permission matter. "Manage files your way" removes both. These are hypothetical facts,
not a default retention policy to insert into a real application.

## Some familiar patterns should remain

A frequency control offering "Daily / Weekly / Monthly" has three real choices; keep the parallel list.
An established label such as "Deploy" should not become "Launch," "Publish" and "Release" across neighboring
controls for variety. A long help article may need headings and a summary. Diagnose wasted information,
not a suspicious word count, punctuation mark or sentence shape.
