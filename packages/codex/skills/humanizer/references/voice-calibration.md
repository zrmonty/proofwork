# Calibrate voice to the person and moment

Use when tone is inconsistent or the request is to make copy warmer, more professional or less synthetic.
Keep the product recognizable. A useful voice is a repeatable relationship, not a pile of personality adjectives.

## Find evidence before choosing a register

Read representative existing action, explanation and failure copy. Separate deliberate product language from
legacy filler. Identify what the audience knows, the terms they use and the stakes of this moment. Prefer
the user's supplied voice examples over a generic style preference.

For broad work, a short working note can name audience, terminology, degree of formality and one action/help/
error example. A single-label change needs no brand workshop or new voice document. If the product lacks
clear direction, choose a restrained, direct register and surface only consequential unanswered choices.

## Keep identity stable while tone responds

A routine save can be brief. A first encounter may need an explanation. A failed or uncertain operation needs
calm status and a safe next step. Humor that works in a welcome screen can trivialize lost work or a denied
request. Warmth can come from useful guidance, respectful phrasing and retained context rather than exclamation
marks or constant reassurance.

An established formal product can remain formal while replacing inflated language with familiar terms.
A technical product can keep "repository," "deployment" or "invoice" when those are the audience's words.
Do not simplify a necessary distinction into a vague "thing" or rename the same action to create variety.

## Express empathy through useful help

State the known problem without blaming or guessing the person's feelings. Explain what remains available,
whether their work is retained and what the supported recovery is. If retention or recovery is unknown,
do not promise it. Keep apology proportional: an occasional service failure can warrant one; ordinary
validation should explain the correction rather than repeatedly apologizing.

Avoid counterfeit intimacy, performative excitement and company-centered celebration. People generally need
to know whether their action worked and what happens next. A genuinely playful product may still use wit
where it does not obscure meaning, exclude readers or undermine a serious moment.

## Keep persuasion honest

Explain an actual benefit, requirement or tradeoff. Do not invent scarcity, social proof, authority, automatic
enrollment or a consequence for declining. Cancellation, consent and account controls must remain legible.
Approved legal wording and required disclosures keep their meaning even when surrounding copy becomes warmer.

## Check across the journey

Read the entry point, control, confirmation, result and recovery together. Stable product terms should survive
the sequence; tone should fit each event. Compare what readers need, not whether every surface uses identical
sentences. When a new register changes risk perception or meaning, revise it before pursuing personality.
